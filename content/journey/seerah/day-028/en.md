# Day 28 - Marked Stones for Travelers Still to Come

## Arrival moment

Some judgments are described in the Qur'an with an almost unbearable precision. Today's is one of them.

## Opening reflection

Once Lut and his believing family were safely clear, the punishment came exactly as promised — total, and specifically aimed.

## Seerah moment

"We turned it upside down and rained upon them stones of layered hard clay, marked from your Lord." Not a natural disaster that could be explained away by chance. A judgment marked, deliberately, as coming from a specific source, for a specific reason.
The phrase "not far from the wrongdoers" closes the verse — a reminder that this same judgment remains within reach of anyone who repeats what this city normalized.

## Tafsir support

"Marked from your Lord" is a phrase scholars note distinguishes this from an anonymous catastrophe. Every stone had a purpose and a source; nothing about it was random.
And "not far from the wrongdoers" is written in the present tense for a reason — this isn't only ancient history being described. It's a standing reality about consequence that outlives any one generation.

## Private reflection

Does the phrase "not far" feel distant to you, or does it feel like it's actually describing something happening in the world you live in right now?

## Tiny action

Make a specific dua today asking Allah to keep you and those you love far from what this city normalized.

## Closing moment

A city was overturned, but the mercy in the warning that preceded it never stopped being offered to anyone willing to hear it. Return tomorrow.
