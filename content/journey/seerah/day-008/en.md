# Day 8 - The First Grief

## Arrival moment

Before you read any further: envy is not a small feeling. It has a history, and it starts here.

## Opening reflection

Adam's two sons each offered something to Allah. One offering was accepted; one was not. What happened next did not have to happen.

## Seerah moment

Qabil could have asked why, could have offered again, could have simply let it go. Instead, he told his brother: "I will surely kill you."
Habil's answer is remarkable — not fear, not a counter-threat, but: "If you raise your hand to kill me, I will not raise my hand to kill you. Indeed, I fear Allah, Lord of the worlds."
One brother chose restraint even facing death. The other chose envy over everything.

## Quran reflection

Read what unchecked envy became — and the lesson Allah draws from it for every generation after.

## Tafsir support

After the murder, a raven shows Qabil how to bury his brother — and he is described as filled with regret.
Then comes one of the most quoted verses in the Qur'an: because of this, whoever kills one soul unjustly, it is as if he killed all of mankind; whoever saves one soul, it is as if he saved all of mankind.
One act of unchecked envy becomes a lesson about the weight of every single human life.

## Private reflection

Has envy ever whispered something to you that restraint had to answer? What did you choose?

## Tiny action

Think of one person whose success has quietly bothered you. Say a sincere dua for them today — nothing more.

## Closing moment

Habil's restraint cost him his life, but it was never wasted. Return tomorrow, gently.
