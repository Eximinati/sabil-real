# Day 26 - Calling for Basic Decency, and Being Mocked for It

## Arrival moment

Some messages should never need to be controversial. Today's story is about exactly one of those.

## Opening reflection

Lut lived among the people of Sodom, related to Ibrahim by family, sent to a city that had normalized something no earlier nation had.

## Seerah moment

"Do you commit such immorality as no one has preceded you with from among the worlds?" Lut's call wasn't complicated theology. It was a plea for basic human decency, and the city's only response was to threaten to expel him and his family as people "who keep themselves pure" — said as an insult, not a compliment.
Purity had become something to mock, in a place that had lost its shame entirely.

## Tafsir support

Notice verse 82's irony: the people's actual complaint against Lut and his followers was that they "keep themselves pure" — using purity itself as the accusation. When a society is deep enough into wrongdoing, decency starts to look, to them, like the strange thing.
This is worth remembering whenever standing for what's right feels isolating: the isolation is not proof you're wrong. Sometimes it's the clearest sign you're not.

## Private reflection

Have you ever felt mocked or isolated for holding a standard that used to be considered simply normal? How did you respond?

## Tiny action

Hold firmly to one standard today that might feel unfashionable, without needing anyone else's approval of it.

## Closing moment

Lut stood in a city that had lost its shame, and did not lose his own. That is exactly what is being asked of you too. Return tomorrow.
